# Deliverables, Coverage and Writing

Select the deliverable before research, independently of A/B/C depth. Preserve the
user's requirements for a sufficiently sourced, readable report. A narrow delegated
brief is a different agreed deliverable, not an excuse to underdeliver a full report.

## Full report: retained source requirements

| Metric | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct cited works | 5–15 | 20–40 | 60–120 |
| Academic share | 0–30%, optional | at least 40% | at least 60% |
| Adequate sources per subtopic | at least 2 | at least 2 | at least 3 |
| Relevant authoritative-source share | at least 50% | at least 60% | at least 70% |
| Sources from last 3 years | 40% recommended | 40% recommended | 50% recommended |

These are full-report defaults, not measured guarantees about what can be read in a
fixed number of minutes. Count distinct cited works after deduplication, and separately
report evidence lineage per key claim; repeated coverage of one origin is not independent
corroboration. Authority is assessed for the claim, not just by domain or brand.

Below a requirement, retrieve relevant evidence if budget permits; never pad citations.
For genuinely scarce topics, the existing exception remains: document the search scope,
what was found, why it is insufficient and the limitation. Do not apply that exception
merely because the time budget was spent; return partial coverage instead. If the default
composition is inappropriate for the requested domain, propose and obtain an explicit
contract adjustment rather than silently dropping it. Recent-source shares are guidance,
not grounds to exclude decisive older work. Above the range, prioritize relevant evidence
and manageable verification; do not silently remove essential contradictory evidence.

## Full report structure

Include:
- Topic, date/revision, scope, depth, source statistics and verification status.
- Executive summary that answers the user questions and can be read independently.
- Contents/navigation when useful for length, then coherent findings and analysis.
- Conclusions, applicability, alternatives/trade-offs, counterevidence and limitations.
- References with traceable identifiers and an appendix for methodology, access/fallback
  records and unresolved citation issues when present.

Explain before comparing. Use narrative paragraphs to connect evidence to conclusions;
use tables for precise comparison and lists for genuinely list-shaped information.
Do not substitute a pile of tables or bullet points for analysis. Do not turn a fixed
50/25/25 ratio, row/column count, or paragraph length into a quality gate. Large evidence
tables can live in an appendix; the main report should explain what matters and why.
Mark illustrative numbers explicitly; do not copy example vendor prices as current facts.

## Methods brief

For the agreed questions, provide:
- Candidate methods/interpretations, assumptions and conditions of applicability.
- Decision-critical supporting and contrary evidence, with source/claim IDs and locations.
- Performance or complexity ranges only where justified and comparable.
- Implementation/validation implications, unknowns and the next discriminating check.

Depth C can mean deep examination of a few decisive sources for a narrow methods brief;
it does not automatically imply 60 references. The brief still needs agreed evidence
coverage and the same key-claim verification discipline. Disclose missing comparisons
rather than inventing methods to fill a fixed count.

## Delta/update

Identify the base artifact, its version and the changed questions. List new evidence,
corrections, invalidated claims, unchanged-but-rechecked claims and unresolved gaps.
Link to retained material; do not imply untouched claims received fresh verification.
For changes to a full report, state which original requirements were re-evaluated and
which remain dependent on the base report. Materially changing the deliverable needs approval.

## Finalization

Review final wording against claim records. A reachable URL is not enough; a failed
key citation cannot receive a green pass because other citations succeeded. State
unsupported assertions as unresolved or remove them and restrict dependent conclusions.
Do not express checklist self-review as a calibrated confidence percentage.

Use `deep-research-<topic>-<date>-<run-or-revision>.md` or an explicitly agreed path;
check for collisions before writing. Keep source/claim records and the receipt alongside
or at caller-specified paths. Avoid exposing confidential source text in a public report.
Return actual paths only after successful writes. Include the common receipt from
`delegation.md`; distinguish execution ending, coverage completion and evidential support.
